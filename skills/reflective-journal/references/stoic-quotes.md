# Base bank of Stoic quotes

A verified subset to start from. **This is not a closed list**: other Stoic passages may be used as long as you are certain they exist and know where they come from.

Each entry gives the reference and an English rendering of the original Greek or Latin. These renderings are written for this bank, not copied from a published translation. When quoting, translate the meaning into the user's language and label it as a free translation or paraphrase (see "Quotes" in `SKILL.md`).

Abbreviations: *Enchiridion* (Ench.) and *Discourses* (Disc.) of Epictetus; *Letters to Lucilius* (Ep.) and treatises of Seneca; *Meditations* (Med.) of Marcus Aurelius.

## Judgments, anxiety, fear

- **Epictetus, Ench. 5** — It is not things that disturb people, but their judgments about things.
- **Epictetus, Ench. 1** — Some things are up to us and some are not. *(The basis of the dichotomy of control.)*
- **Seneca, Ep. 13.4** — More things frighten us than crush us; we suffer more often in imagination than in reality.
- **Seneca, Ep. 5.7** (quoting Hecato) — You will stop fearing when you stop hoping.
- **Marcus Aurelius, Med. VIII.47** — If something external distresses you, it is not the thing itself that troubles you but your judgment of it, and that judgment you can wipe away right now.

## Overwhelm, "everything at once"

- **Marcus Aurelius, Med. VIII.36** — Don't let the picture of your whole life upset you. Don't try to hold in your mind all the troubles that may come; ask of each present one what in it is unbearable.
- **Marcus Aurelius, Med. VI.19** — If something is hard for you, don't conclude it is impossible for a human being; if it is possible for a human being, it is within your reach too.
- **Marcus Aurelius, Med. V.20** — The impediment to action advances action. What stands in the way becomes the way.

## Starting, habits, getting up

- **Marcus Aurelius, Med. V.1** — At dawn, when you are reluctant to get up, keep this in mind: I am rising to do the work of a human being.
- **Epictetus, Disc. II.18** — Every habit and faculty is kept up and strengthened by the corresponding actions: walking by walking, running by running.
- **Epictetus, Disc. I.15** — Nothing great comes into being all at once, not even a bunch of grapes or a fig. If you tell me you want a fig, I answer: it takes time; let it first blossom.
- **Epictetus, Disc. III.23** — First tell yourself what you want to be; then do what you have to do.
- **Marcus Aurelius, Med. X.16** — Stop arguing about what a good person should be like, and be one.

## Time

- **Seneca, *On the Shortness of Life*, 1** — It is not that we have little time, but that we waste a lot of it.
- **Seneca, Ep. 1.1** — Claim yourself for yourself; gather and keep the time that until now was taken from you or slipped away.

## Moving, place, solitude

- **Seneca, Ep. 28.1–2** — You need to change your mind, not your sky. *(About someone who travels seeking relief and takes along what weighs on them.)*
- **Seneca, Ep. 7.8** — Withdraw into yourself as much as you can; spend time with those who can make you better, and welcome those you can make better.
- **Marcus Aurelius, Med. IV.3** — People seek retreats in the country, by the sea, in the mountains; but nowhere does one retreat with more calm than into one's own soul.

## Mistakes, progress, other people's judgment

- **Epictetus, Ench. 13** — If you want to make progress, be content to seem foolish and ignorant about external things.
- **Seneca, *On Anger* III.36** — His evening examination: each night he went over his whole day, hiding nothing from himself, and treated himself firmly but without cruelty. *(Use as a paraphrase.)*
- **Marcus Aurelius, Med. IV.49** — Be like the headland that the waves keep breaking against: it stands firm, and the water calms around it.

## Direction, purpose

- **Seneca, Ep. 71.3** — If you don't know which harbor you are sailing for, no wind is favorable.
- **Marcus Aurelius, Med. II.1** — At the start of the day, tell yourself: I will meet meddling, ungrateful, insolent people… but none of them can harm me unless I allow it. *(Abridged paraphrase.)*

## Avoid (doubtful or false attributions)

These circulate as Stoic but have no reliable source; don't use them as quotes:

- "Luck is what happens when preparation meets opportunity" (attributed to Seneca).
- "Falling is allowed; getting back up is mandatory."
- Motivational lines with no work or passage that "sound like" Marcus Aurelius on social media.

When in doubt: paraphrase the idea, say where it comes from, and leave out the quotation marks.
